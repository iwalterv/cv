# Video #4: What Happened If You Broke the Law in Medieval Times?

**Estado:** v1 (21-oct-2026), borrador para que el usuario lo apruebe **antes** de gastar en voz e imágenes.
**Por qué este tema:** `datos/video4-controversial/README.md`. El usuario pidió algo "más controversial". "Medieval punishments" tiene una mediana de 726K vistas en el top 25 de búsqueda, con 12 videos de más de 1 M, y StickTory (monigotes) hizo 2,1 M (27× su mediana) con el verdugo medieval. Es oscuro e impactante, pero no político.
**Títulos para el A/B:** A) "What Happened If You Broke the Law in Medieval Times?" · B) "Medieval Punishments Were Worse Than You Think"
**Duración estimada:** ~8-9 min (unas 1.350 palabras).
**Estructura:** un solo crimen, el TUYO, desde que robas hasta el veredicto. Cada sección es un paso del proceso, y en cada una hay una salida posible: el público se queda para saber si Red se salva.
**Medidor:** "NECK METER", lo que queda de tu cuello a salvo (100 % → baja o sube según tus decisiones).

**Arranque (regla de la sección 6 de la investigación):** el título se cumple en 15 s (te pillan, ¿qué pasa?), el medidor en una frase, una pregunta abierta (el cerdo juzgado) y otra cada 60-90 s. Giro "controversial": la mitad de lo que crees de la tortura medieval es un invento del siglo XIX, y lo real es más raro.

**Política de anuncios:** nada de sangre ni heridas. Monigotes, cortes ANTES del momento violento, la horca solo como silueta lejana o sombra. Sin palabras gráficas en miniaturas.

**Miniaturas (Test & Compare), estilo v2: fondo plano y claro, una sola situación, 1-3 palabras:**
- A) Red en la picota (cabeza y manos por los agujeros), con un pan colgado al cuello y cara de "en serio" → **"FOR BREAD?"**
- B) Red sujetando con las dos manos una barra de hierro al rojo vivo, sudando, con un cura al lado → **"HOT IRON?"**
- C) Un cerdo con chaqueta y guantes de pie ante un juez medieval con gorro, y Red como testigo boquiabierto → **"GUILTY?"**

Notas: **[Escena entre corchetes]** = indicación visual, no se narra. Etiquetas de voz: [pause], [sarcastic], [whispers].

## 0. Gancho (0:00-0:30)

**[Mercado de un pueblo medieval; Red, flaco y con la tripa rugiendo, mira un pan en el puesto del panadero; el panadero está de espaldas]**
You're hungry. Nobody's looking. And you're about to commit a crime in medieval England.

**[Red se mete el pan bajo la bufanda; un vecino lo ve y abre la boca para gritar]**
[pause] Bad news: somebody saw you. So what happens now?

**[MEDIDOR: NECK METER 100 %]**
That meter in the corner? That's how safe your neck is. Every step from here, it can go up, or down.

**[Silueta de un cerdo con chaqueta y guantes ante un tribunal]**
And stick around, because by the end, you'll meet the defendant who got the strangest trial in history. [pause] It was a pig.

## 1. ¡Al ladrón! El pueblo entero te persigue

**[El vecino gritando a pleno pulmón; la gente sale de las casas con horcas y palos]**
First, your neighbor shouts. This is called the "hue and cry," and it's not optional.

**[Pergamino con sello real: "Statute of Winchester, 1285"]**
Under the Statute of Winchester, from 1285, when the cry goes up, everyone has to drop what they're doing and chase you. From town to town, until you're handed to the sheriff.

**[Un hombre sentado en su puerta, comiendo, mientras los demás corren; un oficial le apunta con el dedo y le cobra una moneda]**
And if they don't? The whole area can be held responsible for the theft. [sarcastic] So your neighbors aren't chasing you because they care about bread. They're chasing you because you're about to cost them money.

**[Red corriendo con el pan, perseguido por medio pueblo, incluido un niño y un perro]**
There's no police force. [pause] There's just everyone.

**[MEDIDOR: 85 %]**

## 2. Si tienes suerte: una multa

**[Una sala sencilla de la casa del señor; un escribano anota en un rollo; campesinos en fila]**
Now, here's something movies won't tell you. Most medieval crime never reached a gallows. It ended up in the local manor court.

**[Primer plano del rollo del tribunal; una moneda pequeña al lado]**
Skipped court? Two pence. Let your cow wander into someone's field? A fine. Brewed bad ale? Another fine. The court rolls are full of these. Thousands of tiny, boring punishments.

**[Red suspira aliviado, guardándose el pan]**
So if you're lucky, this ends with a fine and a very awkward week with your neighbors.

**[MEDIDOR: 95 %]**

## 3. Si eres panadero: la vergüenza pública

**[Una carretilla de troncos (hurdle) arrastrada por un caballo por una calle de Londres; un panadero atado encima con cara de vergüenza; la gente mirando]**
Unless you sold the bread. Because medieval towns took bread very, very seriously.

**[Hogaza pequeña con un cartel "TOO LIGHT"]**
In London, a baker caught selling loaves that were too light could be tied to a wooden frame called a hurdle and dragged through the streets. A record from 1316 shows exactly that happening.

**[Ilustración simple estilo manuscrito: panadero en la picota con un pan colgando del cuello]**
And a 14th-century picture from Bristol shows a baker being punished with the bad loaf hanging around his neck.

**[Red en la picota de la plaza, cabeza y manos atrapadas; la gente pasa y se ríe]**
Then there was the pillory. Your head and hands locked in wood, in the market square, while the whole town walks past. [pause] The real punishment wasn't the wood. It was being seen.

**[MEDIDOR: 80 %]**

## 4. Lo que te contaron (y es mentira)

**[Un museo del siglo XIX, con un señor de sombrero de copa cobrando entrada delante de una "Iron Maiden" llena de pinchos]**
Okay, but what about the torture? The iron maiden, the spikes, all of that?

**[Sello rojo sobre la Iron Maiden: "FAKE"]**
[pause] Here's the uncomfortable part. There's no known mention of the iron maiden before the 1800s. The famous one in Nuremberg was a hoax, and showmen built copies to charge people money to look at them.

**[Red mirando la Iron Maiden con los brazos cruzados, desconfiado]**
A lot of "medieval torture" you've seen in museums is basically Victorian clickbait. [pause] The real system was stranger than that.

## 5. El juicio: hierro al rojo vivo

**[Interior de una iglesia; un cura bendice una barra de hierro sobre el fuego; Red tragando saliva]**
Let's say your case is serious. Before 1215, how does the court decide if you're guilty? [pause] It asks God.

**[Red sosteniendo la barra brillante con las dos manos, caminando unos pasos, con los ojos llorosos]**
It's called trial by ordeal. In one version, the priest heats a piece of iron, you carry it a set number of steps, and your hand is bandaged. If it heals cleanly after a few days, you're innocent. If it doesn't...

**[Un estanque; Red atado, a punto de ser metido en el agua]**
In another, you're thrown into blessed water. And here's the twist: if you sink, that's good. The water "accepted" you. [pause] Floating meant guilty.

**[Muchos obispos en una gran sala, 1215; uno levanta la mano]**
Then, in 1215, the Church's Fourth Lateran Council banned priests from blessing ordeals. Without the priest, the whole thing collapsed. In England, it was replaced by something new.

**[Doce vecinos en un banco, de brazos cruzados, mirando a Red]**
A jury. Made of your neighbors. [sarcastic] The same neighbors who just chased you across three villages.

**[MEDIDOR: 60 %]**

## 6. Las dos salidas secretas

**[Red corriendo y abrazándose a la puerta de una iglesia de piedra; los perseguidores frenan en seco]**
But you have two secret exits. Exit number one: run into a church.

**[Calendario con 40 días tachándose]**
It's called sanctuary. Inside, they can't drag you out. For up to forty days, you're protected and fed.

**[Red descalzo, con una túnica, sujetando una pequeña cruz de madera, caminando solo por un camino hacia el mar]**
But then you have a choice: face trial, or "abjure the realm." That means you confess, lose your property, and leave England forever. You walk to the port they pick for you, barefoot, carrying a wooden cross so everyone knows what you are.

**[Red en la orilla, entrando en el agua hasta las rodillas, mirando el horizonte vacío]**
And if no ship is sailing? You walk into the sea every day, to show you really mean it. Historians estimate hundreds of people a year took this road.

**[Red delante de un juez, sosteniendo un libro en latín, sudando]**
Exit number two: read. Clergy were tried in Church courts, which didn't hang people. And from 1351, anyone who could read could claim to be clergy.

**[Primer plano del libro: Psalm 51]**
The test was usually the first verse of Psalm 51. People called it the "neck verse," because reading it saved your neck. [pause] Some people who couldn't read just memorized it.

**[Un pulgar con una marca de hierro, sin heridas visibles, solo una letra estilizada]**
After 1488, that trick only worked once. If you weren't really a priest, they branded your thumb so you couldn't use it again.

**[MEDIDOR: 75 %]**

## 7. La regla de los doce peniques

**[Una balanza: a un lado el pan, al otro un montón de 12 peniques que se inclina]**
Now let's talk about what you actually stole. Because in medieval England, everything came down to twelve pence.

**[Dos caminos: uno a un poste de azotes, otro hacia una horca lejana en una colina, en silueta]**
Steal something worth less than a shilling, and it's "petty larceny": usually a whipping. Steal something worth more, and it's a felony. [pause] And the punishment for felony theft was hanging.

**[Red mirando el pan y luego la balanza, haciendo cuentas con los dedos]**
One penny could be the difference. [whispers] So, how much was your bread worth?

**[MEDIDOR: 40 %]**

## 8. El hombre del hacha

**[Un hombre serio con capa en una casa a las afueras de la ciudad; la gente cruza la calle para no pasar a su lado]**
If it goes wrong, you meet the executioner. And his life was weird too.

**[Diario antiguo abierto con letra gótica; una pluma]**
Just after the Middle Ages, in Nuremberg, an executioner named Frantz Schmidt kept a diary of his whole career. It lists three hundred sixty-one executions, and hundreds of smaller punishments.

**[Un señor con corona elige a un leñador de entre la multitud y le señala una horca lejana]**
He didn't choose the job. His father was a woodsman, picked out of a crowd by a cruel local lord and forced to carry out an execution. After that, nobody would treat the family normally again. [pause] The job was inherited, like a curse.

**[Schmidt vendando la pierna de un campesino en su casa, con frascos de hierbas en la estantería]**
But here's the part nobody expects. Schmidt was also a healer. By his own count, he treated about fifteen thousand patients. The man people avoided in the street was the one they secretly visited when they got sick.

**[Un pergamino imperial con sello; Schmidt, ya anciano, sonriendo por primera vez]**
And at seventy, he finally got what he'd wanted his whole life. In 1624, the Emperor officially restored his family's honor.

## 9. El acusado más raro: un cerdo

**[Plaza de Falaise, 1386; un cerdo con chaqueta y guantes llevado ante la multitud]**
And now, the trial I promised you. Falaise, France, 1386. A pig attacked and killed a small child.

**[Un tribunal medieval; el cerdo de pie ante el juez; Red entre el público, boquiabierto]**
So the town did what towns did back then. They put the pig on trial. It was found guilty and sentenced to death. [pause] And according to the records, it was dressed in human clothes for the execution.

**[Un recibo antiguo con unas monedas]**
The executioner even filed a receipt for his fee. [sarcastic] Justice, medieval style.

## 10. Veredicto

**[Red en una encrucijada con cuatro carteles: "FINE", "PILLORY", "SANCTUARY", "NECK VERSE"]**
So let's add it up. If you stole cheap, you'd probably get a fine or a whipping. If you got caught selling bad bread, the pillory. If the case got serious, you could run to a church and leave the country forever, or bet your life on one Latin sentence.

**[MEDIDOR sube a 70 %; Red se come el pan, aliviado]**
Your odds were better than the movies say. [pause] But they weren't great.

**[Red mirando a cámara]**
So, which exit would you have taken: the church, the boat, or the neck verse? Tell me in the comments. And if you want to know what you'd have done at night after all that, this one's next.
